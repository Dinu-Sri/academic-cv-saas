The education section of an academic CV does more than list degrees — it establishes your disciplinary lineage, signals research training, and contextualizes your scholarly development. A well-formatted education section helps search committees and fellowship reviewers quickly assess your academic background. This guide covers degree formatting, dissertation presentation, advisor attribution, and coursework inclusion for every career stage.

Education sections follow a universal structure across disciplines and career stages. The section lists degrees in reverse chronological order: doctoral degree first, then master's degrees, then bachelor's degrees. For each degree, include the degree type and field, institution name and location, graduation year or expected graduation date, dissertation or thesis title, and advisor name. Additional details such as GPA, relevant coursework, or academic honors may be included depending on career stage and the specific application context.

## What Information Should You Include for Each Degree Entry?

Each degree entry requires 6 core elements: degree type and field, institution name, location, graduation date, dissertation or thesis title, and advisor name. These elements provide the information committees need to place you within a disciplinary context and assess your training.

The degree type uses the formal name awarded by your institution: "Doctor of Philosophy in Physics" rather than "PhD in Physics." The institution name includes the full university name without abbreviations on first reference: "University of California, Berkeley" not "UC Berkeley." The location uses city and state or city and country for international institutions. The graduation date uses the month and year or just the year depending on convention in your discipline. The dissertation title appears in italics or quotation marks following your discipline's style guide. The advisor name includes the advisor's title: "Advisor: Prof. Jane A. Smith" or "Supervisor: Dr. James Chen."

## How Do You Format Dissertation and Thesis Information?

The dissertation or thesis entry includes the title, advisor name, and committee members if space permits. Dissertation titles appear after the degree line, formatted in italics for most disciplines. For example: "Dissertation: 'Quantum Transport in Two-Dimensional Materials.' Advisor: Prof. Michael Roberts. Committee: Prof. Sarah Kim, Prof. David Liu, Prof. Elena Martinez."

For PhD students and postdocs, include the dissertation title as a standalone line below the degree entry. For senior faculty with decades of experience, the dissertation title may be omitted to save space, though including it helps junior scholars establish their research identity. If your dissertation title is particularly long — exceeding 15 words — consider using a shortened version that captures the core research area while remaining specific enough to distinguish your work.

## When Should You Include GPA and Coursework on an Academic CV?

Include GPA only when it strengthens your application: 3.5 or above on a 4.0 scale for graduate degrees, or 3.7 or above for undergraduate degrees. Omit GPA entirely for senior positions where research productivity and service records carry more weight than academic performance from decades past. When including GPA, use the scale your institution uses: "GPA: 3.82/4.0" or "GPA: 8.7/10.0."

Relevant coursework belongs on CVs for PhD students, graduate school applicants, and early-career researchers applying for positions that require specific methodological training. List 5 to 8 courses that demonstrate specialized knowledge not obvious from your degree title. A chemistry PhD applying to a biochemistry position might list "Advanced Protein Crystallography," "Enzymology," and "Molecular Biophysics." Format coursework as a single line or a compact list: "Relevant Coursework: Advanced Econometrics, Causal Inference, Machine Learning for Social Science, Bayesian Statistics, Game Theory." Omit introductory courses and general education requirements — these add length without adding signal.

## How Do You Handle Multiple Degrees from the Same Institution?

List each degree as a separate entry even when earned from the same university. This ensures clarity about your complete academic trajectory and avoids giving the impression that you omitted a degree. For consecutive degrees at the same institution — such as a BS and MS from the same department — list them in reverse chronological order with full details for each entry.

If you earned a joint or dual degree, use the formal designation provided by your institution: "Joint PhD in Economics and Public Policy" or "Dual Degree: JD/PhD in Law and Philosophy." For joint degrees, include the dissertation title and both advisors if applicable. For dual degrees where each program had separate requirements, list each degree as a separate entry with its own dissertation or thesis information.

## How Should International Degrees Be Presented?

International degrees require sufficient context for committees unfamiliar with the originating country's educational system. Include the degree name in the original language alongside the English translation when the original is in a non-English language. Add a brief note explaining the degree's equivalence if the program structure differs significantly from the US or UK model.

For example: "Diplôme d'Ingénieur (Master's-level Engineering Degree)." Or: "Doctor rerum naturalium (Dr. rer. nat.) — Equivalent to PhD in Natural Sciences." Include the country name in the institution location to provide geographic context: "University of Tokyo, Tokyo, Japan" or "Université Paris-Saclay, Gif-sur-Yvette, France." If your degree includes a grade or classification unfamiliar to international audiences — such as the UK's "First Class Honours" or Germany's "summa cum laude" — add a brief explanation: "First Class Honours (top 10% of cohort)."

## How Does the Education Section Change Across Career Stages?

The education section shrinks as your career advances. PhD students and postdocs list all degrees with full dissertation details, relevant coursework, and GPA if strong. Assistant and associate professors typically list only their doctoral and master's degrees, omitting bachelor's degrees if space is limited. Senior faculty with extensive publication and service records may reduce the education section to a single line: "PhD, Stanford University, 2002."

The key principle is proportionality: the education section should occupy roughly 10% of your CV's total space for senior academics but up to 25% for PhD students and early-career researchers. As your publication list grows and your service record expands, the education section naturally contracts. Never remove degree information entirely — even the most senior academics list their terminal degree as evidence of qualification — but adjust the level of detail to match your career stage.
