Adjunct and contingent faculty face a unique CV challenge: documenting teaching experience across multiple institutions while presenting a coherent scholarly identity. Search committees reviewing adjunct applications for full-time positions understand the structural constraints of contingent work — high teaching loads across multiple campuses, limited research support, and minimal institutional investment in professional development. A strong adjunct CV acknowledges these constraints while presenting evidence of teaching effectiveness, scholarly engagement, and professional growth.

Lead with teaching experience. Adjunct faculty positions emphasize instructional capability above research productivity. List courses taught with institution names, enrollments, and evaluation scores. For formatting guidance, see [how to document teaching experience on an academic CV](/blog/teaching-experience-academic-cv-guide). Group courses by institution when you have taught at multiple campuses. Include a teaching philosophy statement or teaching portfolio link if applying to teaching-focused institutions. The research section should include publications, presentations, and works in progress — even modest scholarly activity demonstrates continued engagement with your discipline. For adapting your CV to different institution types, see [how to tailor your academic CV for different positions](/blog/tailor-academic-cv-different-positions).
