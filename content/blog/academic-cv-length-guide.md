Academic CV length causes more anxiety than almost any other formatting question. "How long should my CV be?" is the single most common question from PhD students and postdocs preparing their first job applications. The answer depends on career stage, discipline, and the specific application context — there is no universal page limit. However, clear guidelines exist for each career stage, and following them signals to search committees that you understand professional norms in your field.

Unlike industry resumes, which typically occupy 1 to 2 pages, academic CVs grow throughout a career. For a detailed comparison, see [academic CV vs resume — the 8 key differences](/blog/academic-cv-vs-resume-differences). A PhD student's CV may be 2 to 4 pages. A postdoc's CV may reach 4 to 6 pages. An assistant professor's CV typically spans 8 to 15 pages. A senior full professor's CV may extend to 25 to 40 pages. This growth reflects the accumulation of publications, presentations, grants, teaching experience, and service contributions over decades of scholarly activity. For a complete overview of CV structure, refer to [how to write an academic CV — the complete guide](/blog/how-to-write-academic-cv-complete-guide).

## How Long Should a PhD Student CV Be?

A PhD student CV should be 2 to 4 pages. First-year students with limited publications may struggle to fill 2 pages, and that is acceptable — quality matters more than quantity at this stage. Include coursework, research rotations, conference presentations, and any publications or manuscripts in preparation to demonstrate active scholarly engagement. The [PhD student CV guide](/blog/phd-student-cv-guide) covers section selection and formatting for early-career researchers.

A third-year PhD student with 3 publications, 5 conference presentations, teaching assistant experience, and a clearly defined research project should fill 3 pages comfortably. A final-year PhD student on the job market should have a 4-page CV with publications, presentations, teaching experience, awards, and professional service. If your CV is shorter than 2 pages as an advanced PhD student, you may be omitting works in progress, conference presentations, or research descriptions that strengthen your application.

## How Long Should a Postdoc CV Be?

A postdoc CV should be 4 to 6 pages. Postdoctoral researchers typically have 5 to 10 publications, multiple conference presentations, teaching experience, grants or fellowships, and mentoring experience — content that fills 4 to 6 pages without padding. The publications section accounts for the largest share of page growth at this stage. See [building a CV for postdoctoral positions](/blog/postdoc-cv-research-independence) for guidance on structuring your postdoc CV.

If your postdoc CV exceeds 6 pages, review whether you are including entries that do not strengthen your application. Remove undergraduate-level achievements unless they are exceptional. Consolidate conference presentations for large meetings where you presented multiple papers — list the conference once with multiple paper titles beneath it. Trim verbose research descriptions to 3 to 4 bullets per project. The goal is a comprehensive record that search committees can scan efficiently, not an exhaustive archive of every scholarly activity.

## How Long Should an Assistant Professor CV Be?

An assistant professor CV should be 8 to 15 pages depending on discipline and publication volume. STEM assistant professors with extensive publication lists, large collaborator lists, and multiple grants may reach 15 pages. Humanities assistant professors with fewer but longer publications (monographs, book chapters) may have 8 to 12 pages. The variability reflects discipline-specific publication patterns rather than differences in productivity. The [assistant professor CV guide for tenure-track applications](/blog/assistant-professor-cv-tenure-track) explains how to structure your CV at this career stage.

The growth from postdoc to assistant professor reflects the accumulation of publications during the postdoctoral period, the addition of independent grants, expanded teaching experience as instructor of record, and increasing professional service. Search committees evaluating assistant professor candidates expect a CV that demonstrates a trajectory of increasing productivity and independence. They do not expect the 25-page CV of a senior faculty member, but they do expect evidence that the candidate has built substantially on their doctoral and postdoctoral work.

## How Long Should a Senior Faculty CV Be?

Senior faculty CVs — for associate and full professors — typically span 15 to 40 pages. A full professor with 30 years of experience, 80 publications, 20 grants, extensive teaching, and significant professional service naturally accumulates a long CV. At this career stage, length is not a concern provided the content is well-organized with clear section headings and consistent formatting.

Some senior faculty maintain both a comprehensive CV (the full record) and an abbreviated CV (a 5 to 10 page summary) for different purposes. The comprehensive CV serves promotion and tenure reviews, grant applications, and institutional reporting. The abbreviated CV serves as a public-facing document for websites, conference materials, and introductions to external collaborators. This two-document approach addresses the tension between completeness and readability at senior career stages.

## How Does CV Length Differ Across Disciplines?

Humanities CVs typically run longer than STEM CVs at every career stage. A senior humanities scholar may have a 35-page CV while a senior STEM scholar with equivalent seniority may have a 20-page CV. This difference reflects the humanities convention of including detailed project descriptions, comprehensive conference presentation records, and narrative course descriptions that STEM CVs present more concisely.

Social science CVs fall between STEM and humanities in typical length. Economics CVs tend toward the shorter end — 3 to 5 pages for junior scholars, 8 to 12 for senior — reflecting the discipline's emphasis on working papers and concise formatting. Psychology CVs run longer, especially for clinical psychologists who include clinical training and licensure information. Anthropology CVs, bridging social sciences and humanities, may approach humanities-length CVs for scholars who include extensive fieldwork descriptions and ethnographic project narratives.

## How Should You Reduce CV Length Without Omitting Important Content?

Four strategies reduce CV length without sacrificing content. First, use consistent abbreviations for recurring terms: "Univ." for University, "Dept." for Department, "Assoc." for Association. Second, tighten research descriptions by removing filler phrases and using bullet points instead of paragraphs. Third, consolidate entries: list multiple presentations at the same conference under one conference heading. Fourth, use smaller but still readable font sizes — 10-point body text with 11-point section headings is acceptable for longer CVs.

Remove genuinely outdated or irrelevant entries. The undergraduate research poster from 2012 no longer strengthens a senior faculty CV. The high school achievement section should disappear after your first year of graduate school. The retail job from college adds length without adding value. Every line on your CV competes for the reader's attention — make each line earn its place.

## Summary

Academic CV length depends on career stage and discipline. PhD student CVs: 2 to 4 pages. Postdoc CVs: 4 to 6 pages. Assistant professor CVs: 8 to 15 pages. Senior faculty CVs: 15 to 40 pages. Humanities CVs run longer than STEM CVs at every stage. Reduce length through consistent abbreviations, tightened descriptions, consolidated entries, and removal of outdated content. Maintain a comprehensive CV for institutional purposes and consider an abbreviated version for public use at senior career stages.
