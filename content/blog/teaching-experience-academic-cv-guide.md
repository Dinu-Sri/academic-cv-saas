Teaching experience carries increasing weight on academic CVs as institutions emphasize pedagogical competence alongside research productivity. For teaching-focused positions, this section may be the most carefully read part of your application. This guide covers how to structure teaching entries, present course development work, document mentoring relationships, and align your teaching record with the expectations of different institution types.

The teaching section of an academic CV documents your instructional experience across 4 categories: courses taught as instructor of record, teaching assistantships, guest lectures and workshops, and student mentoring. Each category follows a consistent format that communicates course scope, your role, student outcomes, and pedagogical approach. The section's placement on your CV depends on the position type: near the top for teaching-focused roles, after research for research-intensive positions.

## What Information Should Each Course Entry Include?

Each course entry includes 6 elements: course name and number, your role, institution and department, semester and year, enrollment count, and a brief description of responsibilities and approach. This structure provides committees with a complete picture of your teaching scope and pedagogical development.

The course name uses the official catalog title. Your role distinguishes between "Instructor of Record," "Teaching Assistant," "Co-Instructor," or "Guest Lecturer." Enrollment counts signal scale: teaching a 300-student lecture course differs fundamentally from leading a 12-student graduate seminar. The description uses 2 to 3 sentences covering course design, pedagogical methods, and student outcomes. For example: "Instructor of Record, Introduction to Psychology (PSYC 101), Fall 2025. Designed and delivered a 15-week course for 85 undergraduate students. Developed active-learning curriculum with weekly problem-based case studies, peer instruction exercises, and 3 scaffolded writing assignments. Average student evaluation score: 4.6/5.0."

## How Do You Document Course Development and Curriculum Design?

Course development demonstrates pedagogical creativity and institutional contribution beyond standard teaching assignments. List new courses you created separately from courses you taught from existing syllabi. For each course developed, include the course title, a brief rationale, the level, and the semester first offered: "Developed and taught 'Introduction to Digital Humanities' — a new upper-division undergraduate course integrating computational methods with traditional humanistic inquiry. First offered Spring 2025 with 24 students."

Curriculum design contributions include syllabus redesign, assessment development, and program-level contributions such as new concentration proposals or departmental assessment initiatives. These entries demonstrate that you think about teaching systematically rather than treating each course as an isolated assignment. For curriculum redesign: "Redesigned the departmental statistics curriculum, transitioning 3 required courses from lecture-based to active-learning formats. Developed common assessment rubrics adopted across all statistics sections."

## How Should You Present Teaching Assistant Experience?

Teaching assistant entries follow the same format as instructor entries but emphasize the mentoring and assessment aspects of the role. Include the course name, supervising professor, your specific duties, and any independent teaching responsibilities. For example: "Teaching Assistant, Organic Chemistry Lab (CHEM 231L), supervised by Prof. Robert Kim, Spring 2024. Led 3 weekly lab sections of 22 students each. Developed pre-lab instructional videos viewed by 180 students across all sections. Graded lab reports and provided written feedback on experimental design and data analysis."

Distinguish between grading-only positions and roles with substantial student contact. A position that involved leading discussion sections, holding office hours, and providing feedback carries more weight than one limited to grading problem sets. If you held both types of positions, place the higher-contact roles first and use more detailed descriptions for those entries.

## How Do You Present Mentoring and Advising Experience?

Mentoring experience documents your supervision of undergraduate researchers, graduate rotation students, thesis advisees, and research assistants. Each entry includes the mentee's name or role, project title, your role as mentor, dates, and any outcomes. For example: "Mentored 3 undergraduate researchers on independent projects in environmental chemistry. Guided experimental design, data collection, and analysis. All 3 students presented at the 2025 university undergraduate research symposium; 1 student's work contributed to a co-authored publication."

Outcomes strengthen mentoring entries significantly. When mentees publish, present at conferences, enter graduate programs, or win awards, these achievements reflect your effectiveness as a mentor. Include these outcomes concisely: "Mentored 4 graduate rotation students; 2 subsequently joined the lab as doctoral candidates." For advising roles such as academic advising or thesis committee service, list the number of students, level, and time period: "Served on 3 PhD dissertation committees (2023-2025)."

## How Does Teaching Documentation Differ for Research vs Teaching-Focused Positions?

Research universities prioritize evidence of teaching competence and efficiency — they want to know you can teach effectively without compromising research productivity. Limit teaching descriptions to 1 to 2 lines per course. Emphasize course variety and enrollment scale. Include quantitative evaluation scores when available.

Teaching-focused institutions — liberal arts colleges, community colleges, and teaching-oriented state universities — require detailed evidence of pedagogical philosophy and student engagement. Expand descriptions to 3 to 4 lines per course covering teaching methods, assessment strategies, and student outcomes. Include a separate section for pedagogical training or certifications. List workshops attended, teaching certificates earned, and professional development activities related to instruction.

## How Should You Include Teaching Evaluations on Your CV?

Teaching evaluations provide objective evidence of instructional effectiveness. Include summary statistics rather than raw scores or individual comments. Report the evaluation scale explicitly: "Average instructor rating: 4.6/5.0 across 4 courses (department average: 4.1/5.0)." The departmental average provides context that helps committees interpret your scores. A 4.3 may be exceptional in a department where the average is 3.8 but unremarkable where the average is 4.5.

Include evaluation data selectively. Report scores for the most recent 3 to 5 courses rather than every course you have ever taught. If you have particularly strong evaluations in a course relevant to the position, highlight that course specifically. If your evaluations show improvement over time, present them chronologically to demonstrate pedagogical growth. Never fabricate or inflate evaluation scores — search committees routinely verify these claims.

## Summary

The teaching section of an academic CV documents instructional experience through course listings, curriculum development, and mentoring records. Include course name, your role, institution, enrollment, dates, and a pedagogical description for each entry. Document course development and curriculum design separately from standard teaching assignments. Present mentoring outcomes that demonstrate your effectiveness as a research supervisor. Adjust detail level based on institution type: concise for research universities, expanded for teaching-focused colleges. Use evaluation data selectively to provide evidence of instructional effectiveness.
