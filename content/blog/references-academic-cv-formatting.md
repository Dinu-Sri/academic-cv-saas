References on an academic CV provide search committees with a verified list of scholars who can speak to your research, teaching, and professional character. Unlike industry resumes where references are typically omitted, academic CVs commonly include a references section with 3 to 5 referees who have agreed to be contacted. This section signals that you have strong relationships with respected scholars in your field and that you are confident your referees will provide positive assessments. For context on how references fit within the broader CV, see [the essential sections every academic CV must contain](/blog/essential-sections-academic-cv).

The references section appears at the end of the CV. For junior scholars — PhD students, postdocs, and assistant professors — the section typically includes the referee's full name, title, department, institution, email address, and phone number. For senior scholars with extensive professional networks, the section may be omitted entirely because their reputation and publication record make explicit references unnecessary. This guide covers reference selection, formatting, and etiquette for every career stage.

## How Many References Should You Include on an Academic CV?

Include 3 to 5 references on your academic CV. Three is the minimum — most search committees request 3 letters of recommendation. Five is the maximum — more than 5 signals that you may not have strong relationships with any individual referee and are compensating with quantity. The standard number is 3 for most applications and 4 to 5 for tenure-track applications where committees expect references covering both research and teaching.

Your reference list should include your PhD advisor, your postdoc advisor if applicable, and 1 to 2 additional scholars familiar with your work from different institutions. Avoid listing multiple referees from the same department unless they can speak to different aspects of your profile — for example, your research advisor and the department chair who can speak to your teaching and service.

## What Information Should Each Reference Entry Include?

Each reference entry includes 6 elements: full name with title, current position, department, institution, email address, and phone number. For example: "Prof. Sarah Richardson, Professor of English and Comparative Literature, Department of English, Columbia University, sarah.richardson@columbia.edu, (212) 854-XXXX." Include the referee's preferred email address — this is typically their institutional email but confirm with each referee before listing.

Include a brief note about the referee's relationship to you only if it is not obvious from their title and institution. For example: "Prof. Michael Chen (doctoral advisor)" or "Prof. Elena Torres (postdoctoral supervisor)." These relationship notes help committees understand the context of each reference. They are particularly important for graduate students and postdocs whose referees serve specific supervisory roles.

## How Should You Prepare Your Referees Before Listing Them?

Contact every referee before listing them on your CV. Send a brief email requesting permission, summarizing the position you are applying for, and providing your current CV and any relevant application materials. Give referees at least 2 weeks' notice before application deadlines. This courtesy allows referees to prepare thoughtful letters rather than rushed responses.

Provide referees with specific information about each application: the institution, department, position description, and any particular qualities the search committee has indicated they value. This information helps referees tailor their letters to the specific position rather than writing generic endorsements. Follow up with referees after submitting applications to confirm they received any automated reference request emails and to express appreciation for their support.

## When Should You Use "References Available Upon Request"?

Use "References available upon request" instead of listing references in 3 situations: when applying for industry positions where reference lists are not standard on CVs, when you have not yet contacted your referees to confirm their willingness, or when space limitations require you to prioritize other sections. This phrase signals that you have references prepared without consuming space that could be used for research and teaching documentation.

For academic positions, listing references directly on the CV remains the standard practice. "References available upon request" may be interpreted as evasive — as if you are uncertain whether your referees will respond positively. Academic search committees expect transparency in references. If you have secured permission from your referees, list them. If you have not yet secured permission, secure it before submitting the application rather than substituting the phrase as a placeholder.

## How Do Reference Expectations Differ by Country?

Reference conventions differ significantly across countries. In the United States and Canada, listing 3 to 5 references on the CV is standard for academic positions. In the United Kingdom and Europe, references are typically requested separately from the CV, and including them on the document itself is less common. In some European countries, references include your relationship to the referee and the duration of your professional connection — information that US CVs typically omit.

For international applications, research the reference conventions of the target country. When in doubt, include 3 references with complete contact information — providing references when they are unexpected is less harmful than omitting them when they are required. If the application instructions specifically state "do not include references," follow those instructions and prepare a separate reference sheet for later stages of the hiring process.
