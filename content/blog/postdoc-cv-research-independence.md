The postdoctoral period represents a critical inflection point in an academic career. Your CV must now do more than list accomplishments — it must demonstrate research independence, funding potential, and readiness for faculty-level responsibilities. Search committees evaluating postdoc applicants look for evidence that you have moved beyond your PhD advisor's shadow and developed your own scholarly identity. For earlier career guidance, see the [PhD student CV guide](/blog/phd-student-cv-guide).

## What Makes a Postdoc CV Different from a PhD Student CV?

A postdoc CV emphasizes research independence, grant acquisition, and mentorship experience — qualities that PhD student CVs address only minimally. The postdoc CV shifts from "potential" to "productivity": publication lists grow longer, conference presentations multiply, and the narrative changes from "learning to research" to "leading research."

Three key differences distinguish the postdoc CV. First, the research section describes projects you designed and led, not projects you contributed to under supervision. Second, the funding section lists grants you applied for and received as principal investigator, not just fellowships awarded to you as a student. Third, the mentorship section documents your supervision of undergraduate researchers, graduate rotation students, or junior lab members — evidence of the teaching and leadership skills required for faculty positions.

## What Are the 9 Essential Sections of a Postdoctoral CV?

A postdoctoral CV contains 9 essential sections: contact information, research interests, education, research experience, publications, grants and funding, teaching and mentorship, presentations, and professional service. The research interests section, placed near the top, articulates your independent research agenda in 3 to 5 sentences. This section signals that you have a clear vision beyond your advisor's research program.

The publications section for postdocs should separate peer-reviewed articles from book chapters, conference proceedings, and preprints. Use subheadings within the section to help committees quickly assess publication volume and venue quality. The grants section lists each award with the funding agency, amount, project title, your role (PI or Co-I), and dates. Even small internal grants demonstrate the ability to conceptualize a project and write a competitive proposal. Professional service includes peer review activities, conference organizing, committee membership, and outreach — evidence of citizenship in the academic community.

## How Do You Demonstrate Research Independence on a Postdoc CV?

Research independence appears through 4 concrete CV signals: publications without your PhD advisor as co-author, grants you obtained independently, projects you conceived and led, and a research statement that articulates a vision distinct from your training environment. Each signal carries different weight depending on your discipline.

Publications without your advisor demonstrate that your work stands on its own merit. If all your publications include your PhD advisor, search committees may question whether your research depends on their guidance. Aim for at least 2 publications where you are the sole or lead author without your advisor listed. For formatting conventions, see [how to list publications on your academic CV](/blog/list-publications-academic-cv). For projects you led, use language that signals ownership: "Designed and executed," "Conceptualized and led," "Secured funding for." Avoid passive constructions like "Was involved in" or "Participated in" — these phrases imply supporting roles rather than leadership.

## How Should You Present Multiple Research Projects as a Postdoc?

Group projects into thematic clusters — 2 to 3 related projects per cluster — rather than listing them chronologically. Each cluster receives a descriptive heading that captures the research theme. This structure helps committees see the intellectual coherence of your work rather than a scattered list of unrelated projects.

For each project within a cluster, provide the project title, a 2 to 3 sentence description of objectives and methods, 1 to 2 key findings or outcomes, and associated publications or presentations. This format mirrors the structure of a research statement and reinforces the narrative of a developing research program. If you have 5 or more distinct projects, consider creating a "Selected Research Projects" section on the CV and providing a complete list in an appendix or online portfolio.

## What Role Do Grants and Funding Play on a Postdoc CV?

Grants signal 2 capabilities that search committees value highly: the ability to articulate a research vision in a competitive proposal format, and the capacity to attract external resources. List every grant application, not just awarded grants. For applications under review, note the status: "Under review at National Science Foundation (NSF), Division of Environmental Biology." For unfunded applications, you may choose to omit them unless they demonstrate effort toward a specific funding goal.

Include internal grants, travel awards, and small departmental funding alongside external grants. A $2,000 departmental travel award for conference presentation demonstrates initiative. A $5,000 internal seed grant for pilot data collection demonstrates the ability to start new research directions. Structure each grant entry with the funding agency, program name, project title, amount awarded, your role, and dates. For large collaborative grants where you contributed but were not PI, list these under "Collaborative Grants" or note your specific role within the project.

## How Do You Present Teaching and Mentorship Experience?

The teaching section of a postdoc CV should reflect increasing responsibility and pedagogical development. List courses as instructor of record first, followed by guest lectures, teaching assistant roles, and mentoring. For each course, include the course name, your role, enrollment numbers, semester, and a brief description of your approach: "Developed active-learning curriculum with weekly problem-based case studies."

Mentorship experience belongs in its own subsection and includes supervision of undergraduate researchers, rotation students, or research technicians. For each mentee, note their name, project title, your role, and any outcomes: "Mentored 2 undergraduate researchers on nanoparticle characterization project; both presented at the 2025 university research symposium." If your mentees have gone on to graduate school or published, include those outcomes — they reflect your effectiveness as a mentor.

## Summary

A postdoctoral CV demonstrates readiness for independent academic careers through evidence of research leadership, grant acquisition, and mentorship. Include research interests, publications without advisor co-authorship, independent grants, teaching, and professional service. Group projects thematically. Present mentorship outcomes.
