Fellowship applications differ from job applications in a critical way: selection committees evaluate potential as much as accomplishment. A faculty search committee asks "What has this candidate done?" A fellowship committee asks "What will this candidate do with our support?" Your CV for a fellowship application must provide evidence of past productivity while framing that evidence as the foundation for future achievement.

Fellowship CVs should be 2 to 3 pages for graduate fellowships and 4 to 6 pages for postdoctoral and early-career fellowships. The document should emphasize research experience, publications, and awards — the sections that selection committees use to assess scholarly potential. Teaching experience and service are relevant only when the fellowship specifically supports teaching or community engagement activities.

## What Do Fellowship Selection Committees Look for in an Academic CV?

Fellowship committees evaluate 4 criteria through the CV: research productivity, independence, trajectory, and alignment with the fellowship's mission. Research productivity appears through publications, presentations, and research experience descriptions. Independence appears through first-author publications, projects you designed, and work conducted without your primary advisor. Trajectory appears through the narrative arc of your research — does your work show development toward a coherent research program? Alignment appears through the match between your stated goals and the fellowship's purpose.

For graduate fellowships like the NSF GRFP, committees prioritize intellectual merit and broader impacts. Your CV should provide evidence for both. Publications and presentations demonstrate intellectual merit. Teaching, mentoring, outreach, and diversity activities demonstrate broader impacts. For postdoctoral fellowships, committees prioritize research independence and productivity. First-author publications, grants you obtained independently, and projects you conceived carry more weight than collaborative work where your contribution is harder to assess.

## How Should You Present a Limited Publication Record for Fellowship Applications?

Fellowship committees understand that applicants at early career stages have limited publication records. Address this limitation by including manuscripts under review, in preparation, and works in progress. Label each with its status clearly: "Under review at Nature Communications," "In preparation for submission to Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences." These entries demonstrate an active research pipeline even when published output is limited.

Emphasize quality over quantity. One first-author publication in a top-tier journal carries more weight than 3 middle-author publications in lower-tier venues. If you have one significant publication, describe its contribution and impact in your research statement rather than padding your CV with minor contributions. Fellowship committees evaluating 500 applications recognize padding and discount it accordingly.

## Summary

Fellowship CVs emphasize research potential and future trajectory over past volume. Organize the CV to highlight research productivity, independence, and alignment with the fellowship's mission. Address limited publication records through works in progress and quality-focused presentation. Tailor the CV to the specific fellowship's criteria — intellectual merit and broader impacts for NSF, independence for postdoctoral awards.
