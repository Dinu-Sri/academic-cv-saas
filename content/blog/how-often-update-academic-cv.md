Academic CVs degrade in accuracy over time through neglect. Publications accumulate without being added. Conference presentations pass without being recorded. Grants end without closure dates being added. Within 6 months of neglect, a CV may be missing 10 to 20% of your current accomplishments. Within a year, the omissions become visible to search committees who compare your publication list to your Google Scholar profile and notice discrepancies. For guidance on maintaining your [online academic profiles — ORCID and Google Scholar](/blog/online-academic-profiles-orcid-google-scholar) — alongside your CV, see the dedicated guide. Regular maintenance prevents this degradation and keeps your CV ready for unexpected opportunities.

The ideal maintenance schedule depends on your career activity level. Active researchers publishing multiple papers per year should update monthly. Researchers in slower publication cycles may update quarterly. All academics should conduct a comprehensive review annually, typically in August before the academic job market season begins. The annual review should verify every entry for accuracy, update dates, and add any accomplishments that fell through the cracks of more frequent updates.

## What Triggers an Immediate CV Update?

Five events trigger immediate CV updates: a publication acceptance, a grant award notification, a conference presentation acceptance, a new teaching assignment, and an award or honor. Update your CV within 24 hours of each event. For guidance on formatting each entry type, see [how to list publications on your academic CV](/blog/list-publications-academic-cv) and the [complete academic CV writing guide](/blog/how-to-write-academic-cv-complete-guide).

The most common update failure is the conference presentation. Researchers attend conferences, present their work, return to their regular schedule, and never add the presentation to their CV. After 3 conferences, 4 to 6 presentations have disappeared from your record. These missing entries represent lost evidence of scholarly engagement — evidence that search committees specifically look for in the presentations section.

## Summary

Update your CV monthly if publishing actively, quarterly if in slower cycles, and comprehensively every August. Add publications, grants, presentations, teaching assignments, and awards within 24 hours of acceptance or notification. Regular maintenance prevents the accuracy degradation that makes neglected CVs unreliable.
