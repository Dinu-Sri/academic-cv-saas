Engineering professor CVs document scholarly contributions that extend beyond traditional academic publications into patents, industry collaborations, technology transfer, and funded research at a scale that exceeds most academic disciplines. Engineering hiring committees evaluate candidates on technical research productivity, funding record — particularly from NSF, DARPA, DOE, and industry — teaching effectiveness in lab and project-based courses, and professional engagement through consulting, standards committees, and technology commercialization.

Create a "Patents and Intellectual Property" section separate from publications. List each patent with the title, patent number, jurisdiction, filing date, grant date, and inventors in patent order. Include provisional patents, patent applications, and invention disclosures if your institution counts them toward promotion. Add a brief statement of significance for key patents: "U.S. Patent No. 12,345,678, 'Method for Real-Time Power Grid Anomaly Detection Using Distributed Sensor Networks,' granted 2025. Licensed to Siemens Energy. Forms the basis for 3 follow-on industry-funded research projects totaling $1.2M." Industry collaborations deserve their own section with partner company, project scope, funding amount, and outcomes including student internships and technology transfer.
