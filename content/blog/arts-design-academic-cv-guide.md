Arts and design academic CVs document creative practice as a primary form of scholarly contribution — exhibitions, performances, commissions, and installations carry the same weight as peer-reviewed publications in other disciplines. The challenge is presenting creative work in a format that academic search committees recognize and evaluate effectively. For comparison, see the [humanities academic CV guide](/blog/humanities-academic-cv-guide) which shares some structural conventions. This guide covers presenting exhibitions, performances, creative works, and scholarly publications in a unified academic CV format.

Create dedicated sections for "Solo Exhibitions," "Group Exhibitions," "Performances," "Commissions," and "Curatorial Projects." Each entry includes dates, venue, location, and a brief description: "Solo Exhibition: 'Displacement Studies,' Gallery 400, University of Illinois at Chicago, January-June 2025. 12 large-format photographs exploring urban transformation in post-industrial landscapes." For design faculty: "Commission: Brand identity system for Chicago Public Library Foundation, 2025. Logo, typography, color system, stationery, and digital guidelines." Include reviews and press coverage of your creative work in a separate section. Document grants and residencies in a dedicated section with the granting organization and award amount. For formatting guidance, see [how to list grants and funding on your academic CV](/blog/grants-funding-academic-cv-awards).
