Senior faculty CVs present a distinct challenge: how to document 20 to 30 years of scholarly accomplishment without producing a document so long that it becomes unreadable. The solution is curation — selecting the most significant and representative entries rather than listing every publication, presentation, and service activity accumulated over a career. Senior CVs communicate impact through selectivity, not comprehensiveness. For guidance on CV length across career stages, see [how long your academic CV should be](/blog/academic-cv-length-guide).

The senior faculty CV should lead with a summary of career achievements: total publications, total citations, h-index, total grant funding, PhD students graduated, and major awards. This summary allows readers to grasp the scale of your career in 10 seconds before diving into the detailed record. The publications section should list the 20 to 30 most significant publications rather than all 100. Group publications by theme or period to communicate the intellectual arc of your career. For formatting conventions, see [how to list publications on your academic CV](/blog/list-publications-academic-cv). Include complete lists in an appendix or online supplement for verification purposes.
