Grant applications require a specific type of CV — often called a biosketch or biographical sketch — that differs from the standard academic CV in format, length, and emphasis. Funding agencies use biosketches to assess whether you and your team have the qualifications, experience, and productivity to execute the proposed research. A strong biosketch communicates research capacity efficiently within strict page limits while complying with agency-specific formatting requirements.

The NIH biosketch is limited to 5 pages and follows a mandated format: personal statement, positions and honors, contributions to science (up to 5), and a link to your full publication list on My Bibliography. The NSF biosketch is limited to 3 pages and includes a list of up to 5 products most closely related to the proposed project, up to 5 other significant products, and a synergistic activities section. Foundation grants may have their own formats. Always use the specific format required by the funding agency. Submitting a standard academic CV when a biosketch is required signals inattention to instructions — one of the most common reasons for administrative rejection.

## How Does a Grant Biosketch Differ from a Standard Academic CV?

A grant biosketch differs from a standard academic CV in 4 ways: strict page limits, mandated section structure, selective rather than comprehensive content, and emphasis on relevance to the proposed project rather than comprehensive career documentation. Your standard CV might be 15 pages documenting every publication, presentation, and service activity. Your biosketch is 3 to 5 pages presenting only the information most relevant to the proposed research.

The personal statement section of a biosketch serves a function unlike any CV section: it explains why you are the right person to conduct this specific research. State your expertise, highlight relevant training and experience, and connect your qualifications directly to the proposed project. This section should be written specifically for each grant application — a generic personal statement wastes the most valuable space in the biosketch.

## How Should You Select Publications for a Grant Biosketch?

Select publications for a biosketch based on relevance to the proposed project, not comprehensive documentation of your career. Choose up to 5 publications that directly demonstrate your expertise in the methods, subject area, or theoretical framework of the proposal. Choose up to 5 additional publications that demonstrate the breadth of your contributions. Provide a brief citation for each and, when permitted by the format, include a link to the full text.

For NIH biosketches, link to your complete publication list through My Bibliography rather than listing all publications in the biosketch itself. For NSF biosketches, focus on products that demonstrate the intellectual merit and broader impacts of your work. For foundation grants, follow the specific format instructions provided in the solicitation — foundation formats vary widely and assuming a standard format can lead to rejection.
