Business school academic CVs differ from those in other disciplines in 3 significant ways: publication quality is assessed through journal ranking lists rather than general prestige, industry engagement and executive education carry weight alongside traditional academic activities, and teaching includes case method instruction and MBA-level course delivery that requires specific documentation. This guide covers the conventions that distinguish business school CVs from those in arts and sciences disciplines.

Business school hiring committees evaluate candidates using discipline-specific journal ranking lists. Finance departments use the Financial Times 50 (FT50) list or the UT Dallas Top 24. Marketing departments reference the American Marketing Association journal rankings. Management departments may use the Academic Journal Guide (ABS list) with 4* and 4-rated journals representing the highest tier. Your CV should communicate where your publications fall within these ranking systems because search committees in business schools evaluate candidates primarily on their ability to publish in top-ranked journals.

## How Should You Format Publications for Business School Applications?

Organize publications by journal ranking tier. Label tiers using your discipline's recognized system: "FT50 Publications," "A Publications" (using the school's internal ranking), or "4* Publications" (ABS list). Within each tier, use reverse chronological order. This organization immediately communicates the quality of your publication record to committees who evaluate journal prestige as a primary criterion.

Working papers receive more prominent placement in business school CVs than in most other disciplines. The "Working Papers" section appears near the top of the CV, immediately after or alongside publications. Each working paper includes the title, co-authors, current status, and target journal: "Under 2nd round review at Journal of Finance" or "Preparing for submission to Marketing Science." Business school hiring committees at research-focused institutions may evaluate working papers more carefully than published articles because working papers represent your current research direction and pipeline.

## How Should You Present Case Studies and Teaching Materials?

Business school teaching includes case method instruction, case writing, and teaching material development that do not appear on CVs in other disciplines. Create a "Teaching Materials" section listing cases you have written, simulations you have developed, and teaching notes you have published. For each entry, include the title, co-authors if applicable, publisher or distributor (Harvard Business Publishing, Ivey Publishing, etc.), and year.

Case writing represents a scholarly contribution in business education. Published cases carry weight similar to publications in some business school contexts, particularly at teaching-focused institutions. Distinguish between field cases (based on primary research with companies) and library cases (based on secondary research). Field cases carry substantially more weight because they demonstrate industry access, primary data collection, and relationship-building with organizations.

## How Should You Document Industry Engagement and Executive Education?

Business schools value industry engagement as evidence that faculty can connect research to practice and contribute to the school's corporate relationships. Create a section for "Industry Engagement" documenting consulting projects, corporate partnerships, executive education teaching, board memberships, and media appearances related to your research expertise.

Executive education teaching — delivering programs for corporate clients and senior executives — carries weight in business school hiring because it generates revenue for the school and demonstrates your ability to communicate with practitioners. List executive education programs with the program name, client organization, your role, and dates. For example: "Designed and delivered a 2-day strategic innovation workshop for senior leadership at Procter & Gamble, 2025. 45 participants. Average evaluation: 4.8/5.0."

## Summary

Business school academic CVs organize publications by journal ranking tiers (FT50, UT Dallas, ABS) to communicate quality to search committees. Working papers appear prominently. Case studies and teaching materials receive dedicated sections. Industry engagement, executive education, and corporate partnerships document the practitioner connections valued in business education. Emphasize journal quality over quantity and working paper pipeline over publication volume.
