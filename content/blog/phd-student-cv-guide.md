As a PhD student, building your first academic CV marks a critical transition from student to scholar. A well-structured CV communicates your emerging research identity, teaching capabilities, and scholarly potential to advisors, fellowship committees, and future employers. This guide covers every section PhD students need, with concrete formatting advice for each.

## What Makes a PhD Student CV Different from Other Academic CVs?

A PhD student CV emphasizes potential and trajectory over volume. It highlights research in progress, coursework mastery, and early scholarly contributions while acknowledging that publication lists may be shorter than those of senior faculty. The goal is to present a coherent narrative of developing expertise.

Unlike senior academic CVs that may span 15 pages, a PhD student CV typically runs 2 to 4 pages. Search committees and fellowship reviewers understand that a second-year doctoral candidate will not have 20 publications. They look for evidence of research direction, methodological training, and a clear scholarly identity. Include works in progress, manuscripts under review, and conference presentations to demonstrate active engagement in your field.

## What Are the 7 Essential Sections of a PhD Student CV?

A PhD student CV contains 7 essential sections: contact information, education, research experience, publications, teaching experience, awards, and references. Each section serves a specific purpose in presenting your academic profile to evaluators.

The contact section includes your full name, institutional email, department and university affiliation, ORCID identifier, and a link to your professional website or Google Scholar profile. Education lists your doctoral program first with your dissertation title and advisor name, followed by previous degrees in reverse chronological order. Research experience describes your doctoral project plus any rotations, assistantships, or collaborative projects. Publications include journal articles, conference proceedings, book chapters, and manuscripts under review. Teaching lists courses you instructed, assisted, or graded. Awards capture fellowships, travel grants, and academic honors. References name 2 to 3 faculty members who can speak to your research and teaching.

## How Do You Present Research When You Have Few Publications?

List publications first, then add a "Works in Progress" subsection for manuscripts under review, in preparation, or in revision. Follow each entry with the current status: "Under review at Journal of Applied Physics," "In preparation for submission to Nature Communications," or "Revise and resubmit at Ecology Letters." Conference presentations belong in a separate section and should include the presentation title, conference name, date, and whether it was a talk or poster.

Research experience entries use the same structure as publications: describe the project title, your role, the methods used, and key findings or skills developed. For example: "Doctoral Research: Nanoparticle Synthesis for Drug Delivery — Designed and characterized lipid-based nanoparticles. Measured encapsulation efficiency using HPLC. Achieved 92% drug loading capacity." Quantify results wherever possible. Numbers signal concrete accomplishment to reviewers scanning dozens of CVs.

## How Do You Format a PhD Student CV for Maximum Readability?

Use reverse chronological order within each section so the most recent and relevant entries appear first. Choose a clean serif or sans-serif font at 11-point size for body text and 13-point for section headings. Maintain 1-inch margins and single-line spacing within entries with a 6-point gap between entries.

Consistent formatting signals professionalism and attention to detail. Use the same citation style throughout the publications section. Align dates consistently — either right-aligned on the same line as the entry title or on a separate line below. Bold your name in multi-author publication entries so reviewers immediately identify your contributions. Include DOIs as clickable links for all published work.

## What Are the Most Common Mistakes PhD Students Make on Their First CV?

PhD students make 5 common mistakes on their first academic CV: omitting works in progress, using inconsistent formatting, padding with irrelevant entries, failing to highlight their advisor relationship, and neglecting to tailor the CV for specific opportunities.

Works in progress demonstrate active scholarship. Excluding them because they are "not published yet" creates an unnecessary gap. Inconsistent formatting — mixing citation styles, using different date formats across sections, varying font sizes — signals carelessness to reviewers scanning 100 applications. Padding with undergraduate club memberships or high school honors dilutes the professional focus. Your advisor relationship matters: listing your advisor's name with your dissertation title signals disciplinary lineage and research context. Tailoring means adjusting section order to prioritize what a specific fellowship or position values most.

## How Does a PhD Student CV Evolve Through the Program?

A first-year PhD CV may be 2 pages long with 2 to 3 publications and a strong coursework section. By the third year, it expands to 3 to 4 pages as conference presentations, teaching experience, and manuscripts accumulate. The final-year CV should closely resemble a postdoctoral CV with 5 to 6 publications, multiple presentations, mentoring experience, and grant applications.

Update your CV at the end of every semester. Add new conference presentations immediately after acceptance. Add manuscripts to "Works in Progress" as soon as you begin writing, then move them to "Publications" upon acceptance. Maintain a master version with every entry, then create tailored versions for specific applications by reordering sections and selectively including or omitting entries based on relevance.

## How Should You List Teaching Experience on a PhD Student CV?

Teaching experience includes courses you served as instructor of record, teaching assistant, or lab instructor. For each entry, include the course name, your role, the number of students, the semester and year, and a brief description of responsibilities. Quantify impact where possible: "Led 3 discussion sections of 25 students each. Redesigned lab curriculum, improving average lab report scores by 12%."

If you have limited teaching experience, include guest lectures, tutoring, or workshop facilitation. These entries demonstrate communication skills and pedagogical engagement even before formal teaching appointments. For PhD students targeting teaching-focused institutions, move the teaching section above research experience and expand descriptions to include pedagogical methods, student outcomes, and any teaching evaluations received.
