International academic applications require adapting your CV to conventions that differ significantly across countries. Information that is standard in one country may be unusual, inappropriate, or even illegal to include in another. Understanding these differences prevents embarrassing mistakes and ensures your application is evaluated on its merits rather than being dismissed for failing to follow local norms. For foundational guidance, see [how to tailor your academic CV for different positions](/blog/tailor-academic-cv-different-positions).

The most significant international differences concern personal information, CV length, references, and the ordering of sections. North American CVs typically omit personal details like age, marital status, and nationality to avoid introducing bias. Some European and Asian CVs include a photograph, date of birth, and nationality as standard — though practices are shifting toward the North American approach due to anti-discrimination regulations. Research the specific conventions of your target country rather than assuming any single international standard.

## How Do UK Academic CVs Differ from US CVs?

UK academic CVs are typically shorter than US CVs — 2 to 4 pages for junior scholars, 5 to 10 for senior — and emphasize grants and research income more prominently. For guidance on presenting research funding, see [how to list grants and funding on your academic CV](/blog/grants-funding-academic-cv-awards). Include the monetary value of grants in pounds sterling. List your PhD examiners if they are prominent in your field. UK CVs often include a "Research Interests" section near the top and may include a brief personal profile of 2 to 3 sentences. For managing your professional identity across borders, see [how to set up your ORCID and Google Scholar profiles](/blog/online-academic-profiles-orcid-google-scholar).

References are usually listed as "References available on request" rather than providing contact details directly on the CV. When references are provided, include the referee's title, institution, and email but omit phone numbers unless specifically requested. UK hiring processes typically request references only for shortlisted candidates, so including references on the initial CV is unnecessary and consumes valuable space.

## How Do European Academic CVs Differ?

European academic CVs vary by country but share some common patterns. The Europass CV format, while officially promoted by the EU, is generally discouraged for academic positions — it is considered too rigid and template-like for scholarly applications. German academic CVs often include a photograph, date and place of birth, marital status, and number of children — though these practices are becoming less common. French CVs may include language proficiency certifications and a "diverse" or "other activities" section for non-academic interests.

Many European institutions require a standardized application form in addition to the CV. Always check whether the position requires a specific format before submitting your standard academic CV. For positions in non-English-speaking countries, provide your CV in both English and the local language when possible, or at minimum include a language proficiency section that honestly assesses your ability in the local language.

## How Should You Handle Country-Specific Reference Conventions?

Reference conventions differ substantially across countries. US and Canadian positions expect 3 to 5 references listed on the CV with complete contact information. UK positions typically request references only for shortlisted candidates. German positions may require "Arbeitszeugnisse" — formal employment references from previous employers. Some Asian institutions expect references from senior scholars with personal connections to the department.

For international applications, prepare 3 versions of your reference strategy: a full reference list for US and Canadian applications, a "References available upon request" note for UK and some European applications, and a tailored approach for countries with specific reference format requirements. Contact referees before listing them for any application and provide them with context about the specific institution and position.
