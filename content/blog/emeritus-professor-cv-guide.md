Emeritus professor CVs document a complete academic career while making space for ongoing scholarly activity. The document serves multiple purposes: a historical record of your contributions to your field and institution, a credential for post-retirement opportunities such as consulting, expert testimony, or visiting positions, and a legacy document for colleagues, students, and institutional archives.

Lead with a career summary: years of service, primary institution, field of specialization, total publications, major awards, and PhD students graduated. This summary contextualizes the detailed record that follows. The publications section should be complete — emeritus CVs serve as scholarly archives — but organized for readability through clear section headings and consistent formatting. Include ongoing scholarly activity: publications after retirement, conference participation, consulting, community engagement, and continued institutional involvement. Emeritus status is a transition, not a termination, and your CV should reflect continued engagement with your scholarly community.
