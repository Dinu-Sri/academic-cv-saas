Medical and clinical academic CVs present a unique challenge: they must document both clinical qualifications and scholarly contributions in a format that satisfies academic medical center search committees. These committees evaluate candidates on 3 tracks simultaneously — clinical competence, research productivity, and teaching effectiveness — and expect the CV to provide clear evidence for each. The resulting document is typically longer and more structured than CVs in other fields, with dedicated sections for clinical training, licensure, and patient care that do not appear on standard academic CVs.

## How Does a Medical Academic CV Differ from a Standard Academic CV?

Medical academic CVs include 4 sections absent from standard academic CVs: clinical training and appointments, board certification and licensure, clinical practice experience, and hospital or clinical committee service. These sections document your qualifications to practice medicine independently — information that academic search committees at medical schools and teaching hospitals require before evaluating your research and teaching. For comparison with standard formats, see [the essential sections every academic CV must contain](/blog/essential-sections-academic-cv).

The education section of a medical CV includes residency and fellowship training in addition to degree programs. Each entry specifies the program type, institution, specialty, and dates: "Residency, Internal Medicine, Massachusetts General Hospital, 2020-2023. Fellowship, Cardiovascular Disease, Brigham and Women's Hospital, 2023-2026." For guidance on structuring the education section, see [how to format the education section on an academic CV](/blog/education-section-academic-cv-formatting). Board certification appears in its own section with the certifying board, certification date, and recertification status: "American Board of Internal Medicine, 2023 (recertification due 2033). American Board of Cardiovascular Disease, 2026." For managing your online research identity alongside your CV, see [how to set up your ORCID and Google Scholar profiles](/blog/online-academic-profiles-orcid-google-scholar).

## How Should You Present Clinical Research Alongside Basic Science Research?

Medical CVs often include both clinical research (involving human subjects, patient data, or clinical trials) and basic science research (involving laboratory experiments, animal models, or molecular biology). Separate these into distinct sections labeled "Clinical Research" and "Basic Science Research" or "Translational Research" when the research bridges both domains. This separation helps committees assess your expertise across the full spectrum of medical research.

Clinical research entries should specify the study design (randomized controlled trial, cohort study, case-control study), the patient population, the sample size, and the clinical outcome measured: "Randomized controlled trial of a novel anticoagulation protocol in 450 patients with atrial fibrillation. Reduced major bleeding events by 34% compared to standard care (p = 0.003)." Basic science entries should specify the model system, techniques used, and mechanistic findings: "Identified a novel regulatory pathway linking mitochondrial dysfunction to cardiomyocyte apoptosis using conditional knockout mouse models and single-cell RNA sequencing."

## How Should You Present Teaching in a Medical Academic Context?

Medical teaching includes clinical supervision of residents and fellows in addition to classroom instruction. Create separate subsections for "Didactic Teaching" (lectures, courses, seminars) and "Clinical Teaching" (resident supervision, bedside teaching, clinical skills instruction). Clinical teaching carries significant weight at academic medical centers because it directly supports the institution's educational mission.

For clinical teaching entries, specify your role, the learner level, the clinical setting, and the frequency or duration: "Attending physician supervising 3 internal medicine residents and 2 medical students on the inpatient cardiology service, 8 weeks per year. Bedside teaching rounds 3 days per week covering cardiac pathophysiology, diagnostic reasoning, and evidence-based management." This description communicates the scope, frequency, and educational content of your clinical teaching.

## Summary

Medical academic CVs include clinical training, board certification, licensure, and patient care sections absent from standard academic CVs. Separate clinical research from basic science research with distinct section labels. Document clinical teaching alongside didactic teaching with specificity about learner level, clinical setting, and frequency. Medical CVs run longer than standard academic CVs — 15 to 30 pages for mid-career physician-scientists — reflecting the breadth of clinical, research, and teaching documentation required.
