The publications section determines more hiring decisions than any other section of an academic CV. Search committees at research universities count publications in top-tier journals, evaluate authorship positions, and verify citation formatting, all within a 2-3 minute initial review. A disorganized publications section with inconsistent citations, missing DOIs, or unclear authorship conventions forces evaluators to work harder to assess a candidate. That friction costs interviews.

This guide covers the complete formatting system for the publications section: organization by type, citation format selection, numbering conventions, author name presentation, DOI integration, and handling of edge cases like preprints, submissions under review, and retracted papers.

## How Should You Organize Publications on an Academic CV?

Organize publications into subcategories by type, peer-reviewed journal articles, books, book chapters, conference proceedings, and working papers, with entries listed in reverse chronological order within each subcategory. (30 words)

The standard subcategory hierarchy, ordered by academic weight:

### 1. Peer-Reviewed Journal Articles

Journal articles carry the highest weight in most disciplines. List them first. Each entry requires the complete author list, article title, journal name, volume, pages, year, and DOI.

### 2. Books (Authored or Edited)

Monographs authored by you, and edited volumes where you served as editor. Include publisher, city, year, and ISBN.

### 3. Book Chapters

Contributed chapters in edited volumes. Include chapter title, book title, editor names, publisher, pages, and year.

### 4. Conference Proceedings

Full papers published in conference proceedings (not abstracts). This category matters most in computer science, engineering, and some applied sciences where conferences are the primary publication venue.

### 5. Preprints and Working Papers

Papers posted to preprint servers (arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN, medRxiv) that have not undergone peer review. Include the preprint server name and DOI or URL.

### 6. Manuscripts Under Review

Papers currently submitted for peer review. List "Under review at [Journal Name]" or simply "Under review" if you prefer not to disclose the journal. Do not list manuscripts submitted more than 12 months ago without an update.

### 7. Manuscripts in Preparation

Use this category sparingly. Only include manuscripts with a complete draft that could be submitted within 1-2 months. Listing "in preparation" papers that never appear as published work damages credibility over time.

## Which Citation Format Should You Use for Your CV?

Use the dominant citation format in your discipline, APA for social sciences, MLA for humanities, Chicago for history, IEEE for engineering, or Vancouver for biomedical sciences. Apply one format consistently across all entries. (34 words)

| Discipline | Standard Format | Example |
|-----------|----------------|---------|
| Psychology, Education, Social Sciences | APA 7th | Author, A. B., & Author, C. D. (2024). Title. *Journal Name*, *12*(3), 45-67. https://doi.org/... |
| Literature, Languages, Cultural Studies | MLA 9th | Author, First. "Title." *Journal Name*, vol. 12, no. 3, 2024, pp. 45-67. |
| History, Philosophy, Religion | Chicago 17th | Author, First. "Title." *Journal Name* 12, no. 3 (2024): 45-67. |
| Engineering, Computer Science | IEEE | A. B. Author and C. D. Author, "Title," *Journal Name*, vol. 12, no. 3, pp. 45-67, 2024. |
| Medicine, Public Health, Biology | Vancouver/NLM | Author AB, Author CD. Title. Journal Name. 2024;12(3):45-67. doi:... |

### Consistency Rules

1. **Pick one format and apply it everywhere.** Mixing APA and MLA within the same CV signals carelessness.
2. **Italicize journal names** in all formats, this is universal.
3. **Use the full journal name**, no abbreviations unless the format standard requires it (Vancouver allows abbreviated journal names per MEDLINE conventions).
4. **Include page numbers** for print publications, article numbers for online-only journals.

The [guide to choosing citation formats for your CV](/blog/choose-citation-format-cv) compares all 5 formats with detailed examples and edge cases.

## How Should You Handle Author Names and Ordering?

Bold your own name in every multi-author publication. Preserve the original author order. Add notation symbols for corresponding authorship and equal contribution only if your discipline uses these conventions. (30 words)

### Name Bolding

Always bold your name so evaluators can quickly identify your contributions:

```
**Smith, J. A.**, Chen, L., & Williams, R. T. (2024). Neural correlates of 
decision-making under uncertainty. *Nature Neuroscience*, *27*(4), 512-524. 
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-024-01234-5
```

### Author Order Conventions by Discipline

| Discipline | First Author Means | Last Author Means |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| STEM / Biomedical | Did the majority of the work | Senior/supervising investigator |
| Mathematics | Alphabetical (no hierarchy) | Alphabetical (no hierarchy) |
| Humanities | Primary author | Secondary contributor |
| Social Sciences | Primary contributor | Least contribution (typically) |
| Computer Science | Primary contributor | Senior/lab head (varies) |

### Notation Symbols

- **Asterisk (*)**, corresponding author (common in STEM)
- **Dagger (†)**, equal contribution (when 2+ authors contributed equally)
- **Superscript numbers**, author affiliations in multi-institution collaborations

Use these notations only if standard in your field. Adding unfamiliar notation symbols confuses evaluators outside your specialization.

## How Should You Number and Order Publications?

Number publications in reverse chronological order within each subcategory. Start numbering from the most recent publication. Numbering enables reviewers to quickly count total output and reference specific entries during committee deliberations. (32 words)

### Numbering Format

```
Peer-Reviewed Journal Articles

[12] **Kim, S.**, Park, J., & Lee, H. (2024). Topological insulators in 
     twisted bilayer graphene. Physical Review Letters, 132(8), 086401.
     https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.132.086401

[11] **Kim, S.**, & Chen, W. (2023). Electronic structure of Janus MoSSe 
     monolayers under biaxial strain. ACS Nano, 17(15), 14623-14632.
     https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.3c02145

[10] Davidson, R., **Kim, S.**, & Nguyen, T. (2023). Machine learning 
     potentials for 2D materials simulations. npj Computational Materials, 
     9, 184. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-023-01123-7
```

### Alternative: Continuous Numbering

Some researchers use a single continuous numbering across all publication types. This approach emphasizes total output count. A CV listing "[47]" as the first entry instantly communicates productivity.

### What About Accepted but Not Yet Published?

List accepted papers with "(in press)" or "(accepted)" notation:

```
[13] **Kim, S.**, et al. (in press). Strain-tunable optical properties of 
     Janus TMDs. Nano Letters. https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.xxxxx
```

Place accepted papers at the top of their subcategory, above the most recent published entry.

## How Should You Include DOI Links?

Include DOI links for every published work as clickable hyperlinks in digital versions of your CV. DOIs provide permanent, verifiable links to the published record that search committees use for quick verification. (33 words)

### DOI Formatting

Present DOIs as full HTTPS URLs:

```
✓ https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07123-4
✗ doi: 10.1038/s41586-024-07123-4
✗ DOI: 10.1038/s41586-024-07123-4
```

The full URL format creates clickable links in PDF viewers. The bare DOI format requires the reader to manually navigate to doi.org.

### Finding DOIs

- **CrossRef** (doi.crossref.org), search by title or metadata
- **PubMed**, DOI listed on every indexed article
- **Google Scholar**, click the article link for DOI
- **ORCID profile**, your registered works include DOIs

### Papers Without DOIs

Older publications, working papers, and some conference proceedings lack DOIs. For these entries:
- Provide a stable URL (institutional repository, PubMed Central, SSRN)
- If no URL exists, omit the identifier - do not fabricate DOIs

## How Do You Handle Special Cases in Your Publication List?

Special cases, preprints, co-first authorship, retractions, non-English publications, and very long author lists, require specific formatting conventions that vary by discipline and career stage. (26 words)

### Preprints

List preprints separately from peer-reviewed articles. Once a preprint is published in a journal, move it to the journal articles section and remove it from preprints. Leaving the same paper in both sections artificially inflates publication count.

```
Preprints and Working Papers

[1] **Kim, S.**, & Park, J. (2024). Phonon-limited mobility in Janus TMD 
    heterostructures. arXiv:2401.12345. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12345
```

### Retracted Papers

A retracted paper must be listed with a clear retraction notice. Omitting retracted papers from a CV constitutes misrepresentation.

```
[8] **Kim, S.**, et al. (2019). [RETRACTED] Title of paper. Journal Name, 
    45(2), 112-119. https://doi.org/... 
    Retraction notice: https://doi.org/...
```

### Mega-Author Papers

In high-energy physics and large collaborations, papers may have hundreds of authors. For these entries, use the collaboration name:

```
[3] ATLAS Collaboration (including **Kim, S.**). (2024). Observation of...
    Physical Review Letters, 132(15), 151801. https://doi.org/...
```

### Non-English Publications

List the original-language title followed by an English translation in brackets:

```
[5] **Kim, S.** (2022). 이차원 물질의 광학적 특성 [Optical Properties of 
    Two-Dimensional Materials]. Korean Journal of Physics, 65(3), 201-215.
```

### Conference Abstracts vs. Full Papers

Conference abstracts are not publications. List full conference papers in the Conference Proceedings section. List abstract-only presentations in a separate "Conference Presentations" section under supplementary sections.
