Conference presentations represent one of the most active sections of an academic CV — one that grows steadily throughout a career and reflects your engagement with the scholarly community. Search committees read this section to assess how frequently you present your work, at what level of venue, and whether you receive invitations to speak at other institutions. A well-organized presentations section communicates scholarly visibility as effectively as a publication list.

The presentations section divides into 3 tiers: invited talks, conference presentations, and posters. Invited talks carry the most weight because they represent external recognition of your expertise — someone specifically asked you to speak. Conference presentations represent peer-reviewed or abstract-accepted contributions. Posters represent early-stage or supplemental work. Organizing your presentations by these tiers rather than chronologically helps committees immediately assess your scholarly standing.

## What Is the Difference Between an Invited Talk and a Conference Presentation?

An invited talk is a presentation at another institution, research seminar, or conference where you were specifically invited by the organizers. A conference presentation is a talk or poster accepted through the standard abstract submission and review process. This distinction matters because invited talks signal external recognition — another department or organization identified you as worth hearing — while conference presentations signal active participation in your field's regular scholarly discourse.

Label invited talks with the hosting institution and department: "Invited Talk, Department of Physics, Stanford University, March 2025." Label conference presentations with the conference name, location, and date: "Contributed Talk, American Physical Society March Meeting, Las Vegas, NV, March 2025." The institutional label for invited talks provides crucial information: an invitation from a top-tier department signals stronger external recognition than an invitation from a regional university.

## How Should You Format Conference Presentation Entries?

Each conference presentation entry includes 6 elements: presentation title, all authors with presenting author underlined or marked, conference name, location, date, and presentation type (talk or poster). For example: "Smith, J.A., Chen, M., and <u>Rodriguez, E.</u> (presenting). Effects of mindfulness training on graduate student well-being. Paper presented at the Annual Convention of the American Psychological Association, Washington, DC, August 2025."

For presentations that later became publications, add a parenthetical note connecting the two: "(Published as Smith et al., 2025, Journal of Counseling Psychology)." This note creates a valuable connection between your presentation record and your publication record, showing that your conference work leads to published output. If a presentation contributed to a publication in a less direct way — ideas from the talk influenced the paper — use "(Related publication: Smith et al., 2025)" to indicate the connection without overclaiming.

## How Should You List Posters on an Academic CV?

Posters belong in a separate subsection from oral presentations, labeled "Poster Presentations" or "Conference Posters." Posters carry less weight than talks in most disciplines, so separating them prevents them from diluting the impact of your oral presentations. Within the posters subsection, use the same format as oral presentations but note "Poster" as the presentation type.

The value of posters varies by discipline and career stage. In some biomedical fields, posters at major conferences represent significant scholarly contributions and are treated similarly to short talks. For PhD students and early-career researchers, posters demonstrate that you are engaged in the conference culture of your field and are developing presentation skills. For senior researchers, the posters subsection should contract as invited talks and oral presentations accumulate.

## How Many Conference Presentations Should You List?

List all conference presentations for which you were a presenting author. For presentations where you were a co-author but not the presenter, include them if space permits but prioritize presentations where you delivered the talk. Search committees evaluating your scholarly engagement care more about your active participation as a presenter than your passive inclusion as a co-author on a presentation delivered by someone else.

For researchers with extensive presentation records — 50 or more entries — consider selecting the most significant presentations rather than listing all of them. Focus on national and international conferences, invited talks, and presentations that led to publications. Regional and local presentations can be consolidated: "Additional presentations at 8 regional conferences (full list available upon request)." This approach maintains the section's impact without overwhelming readers with entries of diminishing significance.

## How Do Presentation Conventions Differ Across Disciplines?

Humanities disciplines value conference presentations more heavily than STEM fields because humanities scholarship develops through oral presentation and discussion before reaching publication. A humanities CV may list 20 to 30 presentations over a career, with detailed titles that communicate the intellectual content of each talk. STEM fields list presentations more concisely, often grouping multiple presentations at the same conference under a single entry.

Economics and some social sciences follow a distinct convention: conference presentations appear on CVs only until the associated paper is published. Once a paper appears in a journal, the conference presentation is typically removed because the publication supersedes it. Other disciplines retain both the presentation and publication entries because they represent different forms of scholarly contribution. Know your discipline's convention and follow it consistently.

## Summary

Organize presentations into 3 tiers: invited talks, conference presentations, and posters. Include title, authors with presenting author marked, conference name, location, date, and presentation type. Connect presentations to resulting publications when applicable.
