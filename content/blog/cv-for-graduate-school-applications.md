Graduate school admissions committees review hundreds of applications each cycle. Your academic CV serves as a condensed snapshot of your scholarly potential — it must communicate research readiness, academic achievement, and fit with the program in 2 to 3 pages. This guide covers every component of a competitive graduate school CV.

## What Is the Difference Between a Graduate School CV and a Job CV?

A graduate school CV emphasizes academic readiness and research potential, while a job CV emphasizes professional accomplishments and work history. Admissions committees prioritize evidence of scholarly aptitude: coursework rigor, research experience, publications, conference presentations, and letters of recommendation. Professional experience matters only when it connects directly to the proposed field of study.

The sections differ in both content and order. A graduate school CV leads with education and research experience. A job CV may lead with professional experience or skills. Graduate applications also require a research statement, personal statement, and writing sample — documents that complement the CV but are submitted separately. Your CV should align with these materials, presenting consistent evidence of the themes developed in your statements.

## What Are the 8 Essential Sections of a Graduate School Application CV?

A graduate school application CV contains 8 essential sections: contact information, education, research experience, publications and presentations, relevant coursework, teaching and mentoring experience, honors and awards, and skills. Each section provides specific evidence of your readiness for advanced study.

Contact information includes your full name, email, phone number, and links to academic profiles like ORCID or Google Scholar. Education lists your degrees in reverse chronological order with GPA if above 3.5, relevant coursework, and honors thesis title if applicable. Research experience describes each project with your role, methods used, and key outcomes — this is the most heavily weighted section. Publications include journal articles, conference proceedings, and manuscripts under review. Relevant coursework lists 5 to 8 advanced courses that demonstrate domain expertise. Teaching experience covers tutoring, lab instruction, and teaching assistantships. Honors captures scholarships, Dean's List placements, and academic prizes. Skills lists laboratory techniques, programming languages, statistical software, and foreign languages.

## How Do You Present Undergraduate Research Experience?

Describe each research project in 3 to 4 bullet points covering the research question, your specific role, the methods used, and key findings. Start each bullet with an action verb. Include the project title, principal investigator or advisor name, institution, and dates. Quantify results: number of samples analyzed, effect sizes measured, datasets processed.

For example: "Undergraduate Honors Thesis: Effects of Microplastic Exposure on Daphnia magna Reproduction. Designed and executed a 12-week controlled exposure experiment with 6 concentration levels and 4 replicates each. Measured reproductive output, mortality, and growth rates across 240 individual organisms. Identified a 34% reduction in offspring count at environmentally relevant concentrations. Presented findings at the 2025 Society of Environmental Toxicology conference." This entry demonstrates experimental design, data collection, statistical analysis, and science communication — exactly the capabilities graduate programs seek.

## How Should You List Publications When You Have Few or None?

List all scholarly outputs: peer-reviewed articles, conference abstracts, posters, preprints, and manuscripts in preparation. Place accepted and published work first, followed by manuscripts under review, then works in progress. Use a consistent citation format — APA, MLA, or your discipline's standard — throughout. Bold your name in author lists so reviewers immediately identify your contributions.

If you have no formal publications, include undergraduate research reports, capstone projects, literature reviews, or data analysis projects completed for coursework. Label these as "Research Projects" rather than "Publications" to maintain accuracy. Admissions committees understand that undergraduates may not have published. They value evidence of research thinking: hypothesis formation, methodological rigor, data interpretation, and scientific communication.

## What Role Does the CV Play Compared to the Personal Statement?

The CV provides the factual record of your academic history. The personal statement provides the narrative that connects those facts into a compelling story of intellectual development and future potential. Admissions committees read both documents together, looking for consistency between the achievements listed in the CV and the themes developed in the statement.

A strong application uses the CV as the foundation and the personal statement as the framework. If your CV shows 3 research projects in different areas, your personal statement should explain how those diverse experiences converged toward your current research interests. If your CV lists advanced statistics coursework, your statement should mention how quantitative training prepared you for the methodological demands of your proposed research. Alignment between these documents signals intentional preparation rather than accidental accumulation of experiences.

## How Do You Tailor a Graduate School CV for Different Programs?

Research each program's faculty, ongoing projects, and recent publications before tailoring your CV. Adjust section order to match program priorities. For research-intensive PhD programs, place research experience and publications before teaching. For programs emphasizing pedagogy, move teaching experience higher. For interdisciplinary programs, highlight coursework and projects that bridge disciplines.

Include relevant coursework that matches the program's methodological requirements. If applying to a quantitative psychology program, list statistics, research methods, and programming courses. If applying to an English PhD, list critical theory, period-specific seminars, and language study. Remove or minimize entries irrelevant to the program: a retail job from sophomore year does not strengthen an application to a molecular biology PhD.
