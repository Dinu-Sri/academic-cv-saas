An academic CV is organized into discrete sections, each documenting a specific domain of scholarly activity. Search committees, tenure review boards, and grant panels evaluate these sections independently, a strong publications section paired with a weak teaching section creates a different candidacy profile than the reverse. Understanding what each section contains, how to format it, and when to prioritize it determines whether an academic CV communicates competence or confusion.

This guide covers all 12 sections found in comprehensive academic CVs, from the 7 essential sections that every academic CV must include to the 5 supplementary sections that strengthen specific applications. Each section entry specifies its purpose, required content, formatting standards, and career-stage variations. For a step-by-step walkthrough, see [how to write an academic CV — the complete guide](/blog/how-to-write-academic-cv-complete-guide).

## What Are the Core Sections of an Academic CV?

Every academic CV contains 7 core sections: contact information, education, publications, research experience, teaching experience, grants and funding, and professional service. These sections appear in every academic CV regardless of discipline, career stage, or institution. (36 words)

The 7 core sections map directly to the evaluation criteria that search committees, tenure review boards, and grant panels apply:

| Section | Evaluates | Weight Varies By |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| Contact information | Professional identity and accessibility | - (always required) |
| Education | Formal qualifications and training | Career stage |
| Publications | Research productivity and impact | Discipline and role type |
| Research experience | Independent research capability | Career stage |
| Teaching experience | Pedagogical contribution | Institution type (R1 vs liberal arts) |
| Grants and funding | Resource acquisition ability | Discipline and career stage |
| Professional service | Citizenship and community engagement | Career stage |

The [complete academic CV writing guide](/blog/how-to-write-academic-cv-complete-guide) explains how these 7 sections work together to present a complete scholarly profile. The ordering, length, and emphasis of each section varies, [section ordering by career stage](/blog/order-sections-academic-cv) covers these variations in detail.

## How Should You Format the Contact Information Section?

The contact information section appears at the top of every academic CV and contains 6 required elements: full name, institutional affiliation, department, office address, professional email, and ORCID identifier. (29 words)

### Required Elements

1. **Full legal name**, as it appears on publications
2. **Current institutional affiliation**, university name and department
3. **Office address**, building and room number
4. **Professional email**, institutional email preferred over personal
5. **ORCID identifier**, 16-digit unique researcher ID ([what is ORCID and why you need one](/blog/what-is-orcid-every-researcher-needs-one))
6. **Phone number**, office or professional mobile

### Optional Elements

- **Personal academic website** URL
- **Google Scholar profile** URL
- **Institutional profile page** URL

Do not include: date of birth, marital status, nationality, or photo (unless required by country-specific conventions, see the [international CV standards guide](/blog/international-academic-cv-standards-guide)). North American and UK academic CVs never include personal demographics.

Format the name prominently. Use a larger font size (14-16pt) for the name and standard body size (11-12pt) for remaining contact details. Center or left-align, both are acceptable. Consistency with the rest of the document matters more than specific alignment.

## How Should You Format the Education Section?

The education section lists all academic degrees in reverse chronological order, including the institution name, degree type, field of study, graduation date, and dissertation or thesis title for doctoral and master's degrees. (33 words)

### Required Elements Per Entry

- **Degree**, PhD, MA, MS, MBA, BS, BA (use full designation, not abbreviation, if the institution uses a different convention)
- **Field / Major**, "Physics," "Comparative Literature," "Computer Science"
- **Institution**, full name, city, and country
- **Date**, graduation year (or "Expected May 2026" for in-progress degrees)
- **Dissertation/thesis title**, required for PhD, recommended for MA/MS
- **Advisor name**, recommended, especially for PhD

### Career-Stage Formatting

For **PhD students**, the education section appears first and includes coursework, qualifying exams, and comprehensive exam dates. For **assistant professors and above**, the education section typically moves below publications and research experience. The section shrinks to essential elements only, degree, institution, year, dissertation title.

Example format:

```
PhD, Molecular Biology                                     2020
  Harvard University, Cambridge, MA
  Dissertation: "Epigenetic Regulation of Neural Stem Cell Differentiation"
  Advisor: Dr. Sarah Chen

BS, Biology, magna cum laude                                2014
  University of California, Berkeley, CA
```

## How Should You List Publications on an Academic CV?

The publications section lists all scholarly works using a consistent citation format, organized by publication type in reverse chronological order. Include DOI links for every published item and mark works under review or in preparation separately. (36 words)

The publications section is the most evaluated section for research-focused positions. Search committees at R1 universities count publications in top-tier journals as the primary metric of research productivity.

### Organization by Type

Divide publications into subsections:

1. **Peer-Reviewed Journal Articles**, full research papers in indexed journals
2. **Book Chapters**, contributed chapters in edited volumes
3. **Books**, authored or edited monographs
4. **Conference Proceedings**, full papers published in conference proceedings
5. **Working Papers and Preprints**, manuscripts on arXiv, SSRN, bioRxiv
6. **Manuscripts Under Review**, submitted but not yet accepted
7. **Manuscripts in Preparation**, near completion (use sparingly)

The [detailed guide to listing publications on an academic CV](/blog/list-publications-academic-cv) covers citation formatting, author ordering conventions, and DOI integration.

### Citation Format

Use the dominant citation style in your discipline:

| Discipline | Citation Style |
|-----------|---------------|
| Psychology, Social Sciences | APA 7th Edition |
| Literature, Humanities | MLA 9th Edition |
| History | Chicago 17th Edition |
| Engineering, Computer Science | IEEE |
| Biomedical Sciences | Vancouver / NLM |

**Bold your name** in co-authored works. Include DOI links as hyperlinks when the CV is distributed digitally. The [guide to choosing citation formats](/blog/choose-citation-format-cv) compares these styles with examples.

### Numbering

Number publications in reverse chronological order. Numbering makes it easy for reviewers to count total output and reference specific items during committee discussions.

## How Should You Present Research Experience?

The research experience section documents every research position held, including the project title, role, institution, dates, and a concise description of methods, contributions, and outcomes for each position. (29 words)

Research experience entries demonstrate the trajectory of a researcher's independent capability. Early entries show apprenticeship under senior faculty. Later entries show independent project leadership and team management.

### Required Elements Per Entry

- **Position title**, Research Assistant, Research Associate, Postdoctoral Fellow, Principal Investigator
- **Institution and department**, where the research was conducted
- **Dates**, start and end (month/year format)
- **Project title or research topic**, brief, descriptive title
- **Description**, 2-4 sentences covering methods, contributions, and key outcomes

### Career-Stage Differences

**PhD students** list all research assistantships, lab rotations, and independent research projects. Descriptions emphasize technical skills acquired and methods learned.

**Postdoctoral researchers** emphasize research independence. Descriptions highlight self-designed experiments, independently obtained pilot data, and publications resulting from each position.

**Faculty members** list Principal Investigator roles, co-PI roles, and international research collaborations. Descriptions reference funded grants, number of personnel supervised, and publications produced.

## How Should You Document Teaching Experience?

The teaching experience section lists all courses taught, course development contributions, and pedagogical training, organized by institution in reverse chronological order. Include course enrollment numbers and student evaluation scores when available. (32 words)

Teaching experience carries different weight depending on institution type. At R1 research universities, teaching is evaluated but secondary to research. At liberal arts colleges, teaching is the primary evaluation criterion. At community colleges, teaching is the entire evaluation.

### Required Elements Per Entry

- **Course title and number**, "BIOL 201: Introduction to Genetics"
- **Role**, Instructor of Record, Co-Instructor, Teaching Assistant, Guest Lecturer
- **Institution**, full name
- **Semester and year**, "Fall 2024," "Spring 2023"
- **Enrollment**, number of students

### Optional Elements

- **Student evaluation scores**, if above department average
- **New course indicator**, "(new course)" for courses you designed
- **Curriculum development**, note if you designed the syllabus, labs, or assessment framework

The [guide to presenting teaching experience on an academic CV](/blog/teaching-experience-academic-cv) provides detailed formatting examples for each role type.

## How Should You Present Grants and Funding?

The grants section lists all awarded, pending, and submitted funding applications in reverse chronological order. Include the funding agency, project title, award amount, date range, and your role (PI, Co-PI, or Senior Personnel). (34 words)

Grants demonstrate a researcher's ability to secure competitive funding, a skill valued at every career stage but weighted most heavily for tenure decisions.

### Organization

Divide grants into subsections:

1. **Funded Grants (Current)**, active awards
2. **Funded Grants (Completed)**, past awards, fully executed
3. **Pending**, submitted, awaiting decision
4. **Internal Awards**, university-level seed grants, start-up packages

### Required Elements Per Entry

- **Funding agency**, NSF, NIH, DOE, ERC, Wellcome Trust, internal university
- **Grant program**, CAREER Award, R01, R21, Discovery Grant
- **Project title**, official title as submitted
- **Role**, Principal Investigator, Co-Principal Investigator, Senior Personnel
- **Amount**, total award and your share (if Co-PI)
- **Dates**, award period (e.g., "2023-2026")

## How Should You Document Professional Service?

The professional service section records all committee memberships, editorial roles, peer review activity, and professional association involvement. It demonstrates institutional citizenship and disciplinary community engagement. (26 words)

Service is evaluated at every stage but carries the most weight in tenure and post-tenure review. Promotion committees expect increasing service responsibility over time, from departmental committee member to national organization leader.

### Organization

1. **University Service**, department, college, and university committees
2. **Disciplinary Service**, journal reviewing, conference organizing, editorial boards
3. **Professional Associations**, memberships, elected positions, committee roles
4. **Community Engagement**, public lectures, science communication, outreach

### Required Elements Per Entry

- **Role**, Committee Member, Chair, Reviewer, Editor, Organizer
- **Organization or committee name**, specific name with institutional context
- **Dates**, "2020-present" or "2019-2022"

Quantify where possible: "Reviewed 15 manuscripts for Journal of Applied Physics (2022-2024)." Vague entries like "Reviewer, various journals" provide no evaluative data.

## What Are the 5 Supplementary Academic CV Sections?

Five supplementary sections, awards, conference presentations, professional development, skills, and references, appear on academic CVs when they strengthen a specific application. These sections are recommended but not universally required. (32 words)

### Awards and Honors

List all academic awards in reverse chronological order: fellowships, prizes, named scholarships, teaching awards, research awards, and honorary degrees. Include the awarding body and year. Monetary awards may include the amount.

### Conference Presentations

List all invited talks, contributed presentations, and poster sessions. Include conference name, location, date, and presentation title. Distinguish between **invited talks** (higher prestige) and **contributed presentations**.

### Professional Development

List workshops, certifications, and training relevant to academic roles: grant writing workshops, pedagogy training, DEI certification, research ethics training, software proficiency certifications.

### Technical Skills

List research-relevant technical skills: programming languages, statistical software, lab equipment, field research methods, language proficiencies. Organize by category.

### References

List 3-5 professional references with name, title, institution, email, and phone. Some applications request references in a separate document. Include references on the CV only when the application specifies "CV with references."

Each supplementary section has its own formatting standards. The section guides linked throughout this article cover these in detail.

## How Do CV Sections Change Across Career Stages?

Academic CV sections shift in order, length, and emphasis at each career stage. PhD students lead with education; postdocs lead with publications; senior faculty lead with their strongest impact area, publications, grants, or leadership. (35 words)

| Career Stage | Top 3 Sections (by position) | Sections to Expand | Sections to Condense |
|-------------|-------|---------|---------|
| PhD Student | Education, Research Experience, Publications | Teaching, Skills | Service (limited expected) |
| Postdoc | Publications, Research Experience, Education | Grants (applied), Presentations | Teaching (if minimal) |
| Assistant Professor | Publications, Research/Grants, Teaching | Service, Mentoring | Education (essential info only) |
| Associate Professor | Publications, Grants, Teaching/Mentoring | Service, Leadership | Research Experience (merged into grants) |
| Full Professor | Publications/Grants, Leadership, Service | Awards, Impact metrics | Education (degrees only) |
