Law professor CVs combine elements of academic and professional resumes, reflecting the dual nature of legal academia where scholarly publication and practice experience both carry weight. Law school hiring committees evaluate candidates on 3 criteria: scholarly potential (demonstrated through publications and research agenda), teaching ability (demonstrated through teaching evaluations and course development), and professional qualifications (demonstrated through clerkships, practice experience, and bar admissions).

The education section of a law professor CV includes law school and any graduate degrees. List law review membership, moot court participation, and academic honors. Clerkships appear in their own section with the judge's name, court, and dates: "Law Clerk to the Honorable Jane A. Smith, United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 2023-2024." Federal appellate clerkships carry the most weight. Bar admissions appear in a separate section: "Admitted to the Bar: New York (2023), Southern District of New York (2024)." Publications follow legal citation format (Bluebook rule) with articles in law reviews listed first.
