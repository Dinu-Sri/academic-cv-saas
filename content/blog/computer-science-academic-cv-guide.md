Computer science academic CVs operate at the intersection of academic tradition and industry practice. Publication lists include conference proceedings as primary venues — a convention unique to CS where top conferences carry prestige comparable to top journals in other fields. Code repositories, open-source contributions, and technical project portfolios supplement traditional CV sections. This guide covers the distinctive conventions that make CS CVs different from those in other STEM fields.

The most critical difference between CS academic CVs and those in other disciplines is the primacy of conference proceedings. In most fields, journal articles represent the highest tier of publication and conference papers are secondary. In computer science, the reverse is often true: papers at top conferences — NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR, OSDI, PLDI, STOC, FOCS — carry more weight than many journal publications. Your publication list should reflect this hierarchy, with top conference papers listed prominently.

## How Should You Format Publications in Computer Science?

Organize CS publications by venue tier rather than chronologically. Top-tier conferences appear first, followed by second-tier conferences, workshops, journal articles, preprints, and technical reports. Within each tier, use reverse chronological order. This organization helps search committees immediately assess the quality of your publication venues without needing to decode a chronologically ordered list where top-tier and workshop papers are interleaved.

CS publication entries include authors (with your name bolded), title, venue name with acceptance rate when impressive, and year. For example: "Smith, J.A., Chen, M., and <b>Rodriguez, E.</b> Efficient Attention Mechanisms for Long-Context Transformers. In Proceedings of NeurIPS 2025 (acceptance rate: 25.8%)." Including the acceptance rate for highly selective conferences communicates prestige efficiently. For preprints, include the arXiv identifier: "arXiv:2501.01234." Preprints carry significant weight in CS because they demonstrate research productivity on timelines that match the field's rapid pace — a paper may appear on arXiv months before formal conference publication.

## How Should You Present Code and Open-Source Contributions?

Computer science CVs include sections for software, code repositories, and open-source contributions that do not appear on CVs in other fields. Create a "Software and Tools" section listing significant software projects with the project name, a brief description, your role, and a link to the repository. For example: "DeepChem — open-source library for deep learning in drug discovery (10,000+ GitHub stars). Core contributor responsible for molecular featurization module and graph neural network implementations. github.com/deepchem/deepchem."

Distinguish between research software (tools developed as part of your research), open-source contributions (contributions to existing projects), and professional software (industry-developed tools). Research software belongs in its own section. Open-source contributions to major projects can be noted concisely: "Contributor to PyTorch (5 merged pull requests, 2024-2025)." Professional software development from industry positions belongs in the experience section with business-outcome descriptions.

## How Should You Handle the Academic-Industry Boundary in CS CVs?

Computer science occupies a unique position where industry research labs — Google Research, Microsoft Research, Meta AI, DeepMind — produce scholarship comparable to top academic departments. Industry research positions on a CS CV should be presented as research experiences equivalent to academic postdocs rather than as non-academic employment. Describe industry research positions with the same detail as academic research positions: research area, specific projects, techniques used, and resulting publications.

For candidates with both academic and industry experience, create separate sections for "Academic Research Experience" and "Industry Research Experience." This separation helps academic search committees assess your suitability for a faculty position that involves mentoring PhD students and writing grants — activities that differ from industry research management. For industry research lab applications, emphasize publications and impact rather than separating academic and industry experience.
