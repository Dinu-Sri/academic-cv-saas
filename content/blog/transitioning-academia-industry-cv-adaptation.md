Transitioning from academia to industry requires fundamentally rethinking how you present your qualifications. The academic CV that served you well for postdoc and faculty applications communicates the wrong signals to industry hiring managers. Industry employers scan for measurable business impact, transferable technical skills, and evidence that you can work effectively in fast-paced team environments — not publication counts, citation metrics, or conference presentation records. For a detailed comparison of these document types, see [academic CV vs resume — the 8 key differences](/blog/academic-cv-vs-resume-differences).

The core challenge is translation, not reduction. Your academic experience represents years of sophisticated problem-solving, project management, data analysis, and communication. Industry hiring managers value these skills but do not recognize them when presented in academic formats. Translating your CV involves restructuring your document around business outcomes, reframing academic achievements in industry language, and removing conventions that signal "academic" rather than "professional."

## How Should You Restructure Your Academic CV for Industry Roles?

Replace the academic CV structure with an industry resume structure. Lead with a professional summary — 3 to 4 sentences describing your expertise and the value you bring to organizations. Follow with a skills section organized by category: technical skills, analytical methods, project management, and communication. Place professional experience next, describing research roles as jobs with measurable outcomes. Move education to the end, listing only degrees and institutions without dissertation titles or advisor names unless directly relevant to the target role.

Remove sections that industry hiring managers do not evaluate: publications (replace with "Selected Publications" listing 3 to 5 most relevant papers), conference presentations, academic service, teaching experience (unless applying to education or training roles), and references. For guidance on tailoring applications to different sectors, see [how to tailor your academic CV for different positions](/blog/tailor-academic-cv-different-positions). Add sections that industry employers expect: project management experience, cross-functional collaboration, budget responsibility, and direct reports supervised. The document should occupy 2 pages maximum — industry hiring managers spend an average of 6 to 10 seconds on initial resume screening compared to the 30 to 60 seconds academic committees spend on CVs.

## How Do You Translate Research into Business Outcomes?

Replace research descriptions from academic language (publications, methods, findings) into business language (problems solved, methods used, business impact). For guidance on which technical competencies to highlight, see [how to present the skills section on your academic CV](/blog/skills-section-academic-cv). Replace "Published 8 peer-reviewed articles on machine learning applications in healthcare" with "Developed machine learning models that reduced patient readmission rates by 22%, saving an estimated $1.2M annually at a 400-bed hospital system." The academic version emphasizes publication count. The business version emphasizes measurable financial and operational impact.

Use the STAR framework — Situation, Task, Action, Result — to structure each experience entry. Situation: what problem existed? Task: what was your responsibility? Action: what did you do? Result: what measurable outcome occurred? For example: "Situation: Manufacturing quality control process had a 3.5% defect rate, exceeding the 2% threshold. Task: Lead a data science team to identify root causes and develop predictive models. Action: Analyzed 18 months of production data across 12 assembly lines. Built random forest classifier to predict defect probability from sensor data. Result: Implemented model-driven quality interventions that reduced defect rate to 1.7%, saving $340,000 annually."

## Which Academic Conventions Should You Remove for Industry Applications?

Remove 5 academic conventions from industry resumes: publication lists beyond a brief "Selected Publications" section, conference presentations, academic references, GPA and coursework for experienced professionals, and academic jargon. Replace academic references with professional references from industry collaborators, internship supervisors, or consulting clients. Replace discipline-specific jargon with accessible language that communicates expertise to non-specialist readers.

Remove the "PhD" title from your name unless you are applying to research scientist positions where doctoral credentials are expected. In many industry contexts, "PhD" signals "overqualified academic who will struggle to adapt to business culture." Lead with your professional identity rather than your academic credential. The same person who is "Dr. Jane Smith, PhD" on an academic CV becomes "Jane Smith, Data Scientist" on an industry resume.

## How Should You Present Technical Skills for Industry Roles?

Organize technical skills by category with proficiency levels. Include programming languages, analytical tools, laboratory techniques, project management methodologies, and communication platforms. Be specific: "Python (pandas, scikit-learn, TensorFlow), SQL, Tableau" rather than "data analysis." Industry hiring managers and applicant tracking systems scan for specific keywords. Vague skill descriptions fail both human and automated screening.

Include soft skills that academics often omit because academic CVs demonstrate them implicitly: project management, team leadership, stakeholder communication, budget management, and mentoring. Industry hiring managers look for explicit evidence of these skills because they indicate readiness for the collaborative, deadline-driven environment of business. Quantify where possible: "Managed a $150,000 research budget across 3 projects with 4 direct reports" communicates skills that "Responsible for laboratory budget and personnel" does not.

## Summary

Transitioning from academia to industry requires translating your CV from an academic document into a business document. Lead with a professional summary and skills section. Restructure research descriptions as business outcomes using the STAR framework. Remove academic conventions: extensive publication lists, conference presentations, academic references, and jargon. Present technical skills with specific keywords and proficiency levels. Include soft skills with quantified evidence. Keep the document to 2 pages.
