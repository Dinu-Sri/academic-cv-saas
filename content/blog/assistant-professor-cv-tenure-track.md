The assistant professor CV represents the most consequential document in an academic career. It must demonstrate research independence, teaching competence, and scholarly potential to search committees that review 100 to 300 applications for a single position. Unlike PhD student or postdoc CVs that emphasize potential and trajectory, the assistant professor CV must show accomplished scholarship with clear evidence of a developing independent research program. For earlier career stages, see the [PhD student CV guide](/blog/phd-student-cv-guide) and [postdoc CV guide](/blog/postdoc-cv-research-independence).

The assistant professor job market has grown increasingly competitive. The number of PhD graduates in the United States exceeded 57,000 in 2023, while tenure-track positions have remained relatively stable, creating ratios of 100 to 300 applicants per position in many fields. Your CV must distinguish you from candidates with comparable qualifications through strategic organization, precise language, and clear evidence of the qualities search committees value most.

## What Are the 10 Essential Sections of an Assistant Professor CV?

An assistant professor CV contains 10 essential sections ordered by institutional priority: contact information, research interests, education, research experience, publications, grants and funding, teaching experience, presentations, professional service, and references. Research-focused institutions expect the research sections to occupy 60% of the first 3 pages. Teaching-focused institutions expect more balanced coverage with teaching experience placed immediately after research.

The contact section includes your name, institutional affiliation, email, ORCID, and links to your professional website and Google Scholar. Research interests appears as a 4 to 6 sentence paragraph articulating your research program — past accomplishments, current projects, and future directions. For guidance on crafting this section, see [how to write a personal statement and research summary on your academic CV](/blog/academic-cv-personal-statement-research-summary). Education lists your doctoral degree first with dissertation title and advisor. Research experience describes your postdoctoral and doctoral projects with specific methods and outcomes. Publications separate peer-reviewed articles from other output types. For formatting standards, see [how to list publications on your academic CV](/blog/list-publications-academic-cv). Grants lists competitive funding with amounts and your role. For guidance on presenting funding, see [how to list grants and awards on your academic CV](/blog/grants-funding-academic-cv-awards). Teaching includes courses taught, enrollments, and evaluation scores. Presentations separates invited talks from conference contributions. Service documents departmental and disciplinary contributions. References lists 3 to 5 scholars who can speak to your research and teaching.

## How Should You Present Your Research Program for a Tenure-Track Search?

The research section of an assistant professor CV must communicate 3 things: what you have accomplished, what you are working on now, and what you plan to do next. Search committees evaluate this section as evidence of a sustainable, fundable research program that will generate publications, attract graduate students, and contribute to the department's scholarly profile over a 6-year tenure clock. For guidance on documenting achievements for promotion, see [building a tenure portfolio and dossier](/blog/tenure-portfolio-dossier-guide).

Structure your research description chronologically within thematic groupings. Postdoctoral research appears first, followed by doctoral research. For each project, describe the research question, your approach, key findings, and resulting publications. For example: "Postdoctoral Research: Neural Mechanisms of Decision-Making Under Uncertainty. Developed a computational model integrating reinforcement learning with Bayesian inference to predict human choices in dynamic environments. Tested model predictions using fMRI in 42 participants. Identified distinct neural signatures for expected value computation and uncertainty representation in prefrontal and parietal cortices. Resulted in 3 first-author publications including one in *Neuron* and one in *Nature Neuroscience*." This entry communicates the research question, methods, sample size, analytical approach, key finding, and publication outcomes — all in 4 sentences.

## How Should You Integrate Your Research Statement with Your CV?

The research statement and CV function as complementary documents in a tenure-track application. The research statement provides the narrative — the intellectual motivation behind your work, the connections between projects, and the vision for your future research program. The CV provides the evidence — the publications, grants, presentations, and collaborations that substantiate the claims in your statement. Search committees read both documents together, checking for consistency and gap.

Align your CV with your research statement in 3 ways. First, order your research projects on the CV to match the narrative arc of your statement. If your statement opens with your postdoctoral work, place that project first on the CV. Second, use consistent project titles across both documents. A project called "Neural Mechanisms of Decision-Making Under Uncertainty" on your CV should not appear as "Decision Neuroscience Research" in your statement. Third, cross-reference your statement's claims in the CV. If your statement mentions that your work resulted in a specific grant, that grant must appear in your CV's funding section with matching details.

## How Should You Present Teaching for an Assistant Professor Position?

Teaching experience on an assistant professor CV demonstrates that you can handle the instructional responsibilities of a faculty position — typically 2 to 3 courses per year at research universities, 3 to 4 at comprehensive universities, and 4 to 5 at community colleges. Adjust the teaching section's length and detail to match the institution's teaching expectations.

For each course, include the course name, your role, enrollment, semester, and a 1 to 2 line description of your approach and outcomes. For example: "Instructor of Record, Cognitive Psychology (PSYC 240), Spring 2025. Designed and delivered a 15-week course for 65 undergraduates. Developed active-learning curriculum with weekly laboratory exercises and team-based research projects. Average evaluation: 4.7/5.0." Include a separate subsection for teaching development activities: pedagogy workshops, course design institutes, teaching certificates, and observations of your teaching by faculty mentors.

## How Should You Handle a Limited Publication Record?

Assistant professor candidates in some fields — particularly humanities disciplines where monograph publication takes years and experimental sciences where data collection spans multiple years — may have relatively short publication lists. Address this honestly by including works in progress, manuscripts under review, and a clear pipeline of forthcoming work.

Quality matters more than quantity at the assistant professor level. A search committee evaluating a candidate with 3 publications in top-tier journals rates that candidate higher than one with 8 publications in lower-tier venues. Do not inflate your publication count with tangential contributions. Focus on the publications that represent your best and most relevant work. If you have co-authored publications, ensure your contribution is clearly described either in the publication entry or in the research experience section where the project is described in detail.

The assistant professor CV must demonstrate research independence, teaching competence, and scholarly potential through 10 essential sections ordered by institutional priority. Present research with thematic groupings that communicate past accomplishments, current projects, and future directions.
